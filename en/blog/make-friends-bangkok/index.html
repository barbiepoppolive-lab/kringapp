How to Make Friends in Bangkok as an Adult: 5 Ways That Work

The fastest way to make friends in Bangkok as an adult is to pick one activity that runs every week and keep showing up, so the same faces become familiar. Add one friend-first app and two or three regular spots near home, and a small circle tends to follow.

By the Kring team · Updated 17 Nov 2026 · 4 min read

✓ Facts on this page last checked 17 Nov 2026 · we re-check this page every 3 months

Why it feels harder after your twenties

At school or university you saw the same people every day without trying. Once you start working, that structure disappears. Friends move away, schedules clash, and Bangkok's size and traffic mean most people stay inside their own part of the city after work.

The good news is that this is not about personality. Friendship mostly comes from repeated, low-pressure contact with the same people. Rebuild that and the rest gets much easier.

1. Choose a weekly thing, not a one-off event

One-off events are fun, but very few friendships start from a single evening. Weekly activities work better: by the third time people know your name, and by the fifth someone suggests food afterwards.

  • Run clubs. Several are free and need no sign-up. SarDine Run Club, for example, runs on Saturdays and rotates between Suan Rot Fai, Lumphini Park and Benjakitti Park (per Time Out, February 2026). More in running in Bangkok.
  • Board-game cafés. A game gives everyone something to talk about from minute one. See board-game cafés in Bangkok.
  • Courses, not one-day workshops. Muay Thai, pottery, cooking or Thai language classes put you with the same classmates week after week.

2. Make your neighbourhood smaller

People with friends nearby are usually regulars somewhere. Choose two or three places close to the BTS or MRT station you use every day: one café, one park, one co-working space. Go at similar times. Staff start to recognise you, and so do the other regulars.

If you are still choosing an area, Ari has walkable, leafy sois and a relaxed crowd, and Talat Noi is great for slow afternoons by the river.

3. Use apps built for friends

Apps for finding a partner are a poor tool for this. Better options are apps designed for friendship: Bumble BFF, rebuilt in 2025 with a focus on groups; Timeleft, which seats four to six strangers at a dinner table and lists Bangkok among its cities; and hobby groups on LINE OpenChat, which almost every Thai already uses. We compare them honestly in best apps to make friends in Bangkok.

4. Say yes, then suggest the next plan

Most people want more friends; they are just waiting for someone else to start. When a conversation clicks, make a specific offer: "Lumphini Park run, Saturday at 7?" beats "we should hang out sometime", which usually dies in the chat.

Small, cheap plans in public places are the easiest to say yes to. Inviting three or four people at once also takes the pressure off, and it fits how Thai friends often hang out anyway: in groups.

Openers that actually work

You do not need clever lines. Simple questions tied to what is in front of you work best.

  • At a run club: "Do you come every week? Is this pace okay for you?"
  • At a board-game table: "How does this one work? Can you teach me?"
  • At your regular café: "I see you here a lot, do you work nearby?"

Ask questions that need more than a yes or no, then genuinely listen. People remember who listened far longer than who was funny.

5. Keep it safe and easy

  • Make the first plans in public places, in daytime or early evening.
  • Tell a friend where you are going and with whom.
  • Get there and back on your own.
  • Keep your home address and exact workplace to yourself until you know people well.

If you have just moved here for work

The first year in a new city is lonely for a lot of people, Thai and foreign alike. Start with things you can do alone but around others: an early run in the park, a co-working desk, an evening language class. Once you keep seeing the same faces, saying hello stops feeling like a big step.

FAQ

Is it normal to have no friends in Bangkok as an adult?

Very normal, especially after graduating or moving for work. Daily life stops putting you in front of the same people. The fix is a routine that does: the same activity, every week.

Where can I make friends in Bangkok for free?

Several run clubs are free, public parks such as Lumphini and Benjakitti cost nothing to enter, and the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre has free exhibitions and regular public events.

How long does it take to make close friends?

There is no fixed number, but frequency matters most. Going to the same weekly activity beats attending many different one-off events.
