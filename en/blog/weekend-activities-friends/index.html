Weekend Activities to Make Friends in Bangkok (2027)

The easiest weekend activities for making friends in Bangkok are morning run clubs, afternoon board-game cafés and language exchanges, because many people come alone and they repeat every week. Volunteering and park cycling are great if you would rather not start conversations cold.

By the Kring team · Updated 17 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Why recurring beats one-off

A weekly activity puts you in front of the same people again and again, which is how acquaintances become friends. Everything below is real and sourced at the bottom, but schedules change, so check each group's Instagram or page before you go.

Saturday morning: run clubs

  • SarDine Run Club runs on Saturdays, free, with no sign-up, rotating between Suan Rot Fai, Lumphini Park and Benjakitti Park.
  • Ugly Running Training Club (URT) runs most mornings around 6:30am at Gate 3 of Benjakitti Park, by the stretching zone.
  • Sabai Run Club has Sunday-morning and Friday-night runs with big turnouts.

All per Time Out, February 2026. You do not need to be fast; many groups have easy-pace options. More in running in Bangkok.

Any weekend: cycling at Suan Rot Fai

Wachirabenchathat Park (Suan Rot Fai), near Chatuchak, has bike rental, shaded cycle paths and a butterfly garden, and it gets busy at weekends. Riding as a small group of new friends is relaxed, because you do not have to talk the whole time.

Saturday afternoon: board-game cafés

Games get strangers talking immediately. The Thaiger's October 2025 picks include The Stronghold in Siam Square Soi 9, Quest on the 7th floor of MBK Center (about 60 baht per person per hour) and Turn The Tables near Victory Monument. Details in board-game cafés in Bangkok.

Sunday afternoon: language exchange

Thai–English language exchanges have a long history in Bangkok. Many gather in cafés or central parks, often free, splitting time between Thai and English. Groups come and go, so find one that is still active on Facebook or LINE OpenChat first. Thais practise English, visitors practise Thai, and everyone leaves with cross-cultural friends. Schedules change often, so check the group's latest listing first.

Workshops and courses

Pottery, candle-making, cooking or a Muay Thai course puts you with the same classmates for several weeks. Ari and Charoen Krung both have small studios; search Instagram for the area name plus "workshop".

Volunteering: friends with a purpose

Scholars of Sustenance (SOS) works on food waste. Its Rescue Kitchen programme cooks surplus ingredients from businesses into meals delivered to low-income communities in Bangkok, and you can contact them through their website to volunteer. Working as a team with strangers builds closeness faster than you would expect.

A sample Saturday

  • 07:00 run with a club, then breakfast with the group
  • 11:00 head home and hide from the midday heat
  • 14:00 board-game café around Siam
  • 17:30 wander the Bangkok Art and Culture Centre (open until 20:00)

You do not need to do it all. Pick one and repeat it every week.

How to show up the first time

  • Arrive ten minutes early. It is easier to talk before everyone has formed little groups.
  • Introduce yourself to the organiser or group admin; they often introduce newcomers around.
  • Say hello to someone else who looks like they came alone. They want someone to talk to as much as you do.
  • Before you leave, swap LINE or Instagram with anyone you clicked with, and say you will be back next week.

FAQ

What can I do on weekends in Bangkok to make friends?

Morning run clubs, afternoon board-game cafés, language exchanges, multi-week courses and volunteering all repeat regularly and attract lots of people who come alone.

Do Bangkok run clubs cost money?

Many are free with no sign-up, such as SarDine Run Club per Time Out (February 2026), though some special events need pre-registration.

Can I rent a bike at Suan Rot Fai?

Yes. Wachirabenchathat Park (Suan Rot Fai) has bike rental, and it gets busy at weekends.

How can I volunteer in Bangkok?

Start with organisations that have clear programmes, such as Scholars of Sustenance, whose Rescue Kitchen delivers meals to low-income communities.
