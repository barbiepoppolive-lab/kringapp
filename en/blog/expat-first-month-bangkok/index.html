Your First Month in Bangkok: A Newcomer's Checklist

In your first month in Bangkok, get three things done before anything else: a Thai number with the apps Thais actually use, the rail line that serves your home, and one weekly routine where you see the same people. The rest follows from there.

By the Kring team · Updated 17 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

✓ Facts on this page last checked 17 Nov 2026 · we re-check this page every 3 months

Week 1: number, apps, transport

  • SIM card. Buy one at the airport or a mobile operator's shop. You need your passport to register it.
  • LINE. Thailand's main messaging app. Shops, condo offices and colleagues will almost all contact you on LINE.
  • Ride-hailing and delivery apps, such as Grab and Bolt, help a lot while you are still learning the map.
  • Rail. The MRT accepts contactless Visa and Mastercard plus certain debit and prepaid cards; the old MRT stored-value cards stopped opening the gates on 1 June 2026. The BTS has its own card system, so ask at the ticket office.

Week 2: a place to live

  • Choose somewhere near a BTS or MRT station first, then worry about room size. Traffic eats more time than you expect.
  • Most leases ask for a deposit plus rent in advance. Read the whole contract and photograph every corner of the room before you move in.
  • Ask exactly how electricity and water are billed; some buildings set their own prices.
  • Foreigners' addresses must be reported to immigration (the "TM30" notification). The landlord normally files it, but ask to make sure it is done.

Before you pay a deposit

Fake listings are common in Facebook rental groups. Never transfer a deposit before you have seen the room in person and spoken to the landlord or the building office, and only pay into an account in the owner's or the building company's name.

Week 3: daily life

  • Pick one regular spot near home: a coffee shop, a laundry, a noodle stall. Being recognised makes a neighbourhood feel like yours much faster.
  • Walk your nearest park early in the morning. Lumphini Park opens at 04:30, and dawn shows a side of Bangkok most visitors never see.
  • Learn a handful of Thai phrases. Start with Thai phrases and etiquette for making Thai friends.

Week 4: make friends on purpose

The first month is usually the loneliest, which is exactly why to set up a social routine now.

  • Choose one weekly activity: a Saturday run club, board games or a language exchange. See weekend activities to make friends.
  • Try an expat network such as InterNations if you have moved from abroad.
  • Use at least one friend app. See best apps to make friends in Bangkok.

Common newcomer mistakes

  • Underestimating travel time, especially on Friday evenings and rainy days.
  • Going out between noon and 3pm in the hot season.
  • Living entirely in apps and never joining anything in person.
  • Expecting to feel settled in days, not months.

Pick the right first area

If you are unsure, two easy places to start are Ari, which is walkable with a real neighbourhood feel, and around Siam, the best-connected part of the city.

When something goes wrong

For emergencies, Thailand's 1155 police helpline for visitors has English-speaking staff, and general emergency numbers are 191 for police and 1669 for ambulance. Save them in your phone on day one, along with your embassy's number.

Let people help you

Bangkok people are generally very willing to help a newcomer who asks politely. Your condo office probably has a LINE group for residents; join it, because that is where water cuts, deliveries and building news are announced. When you are stuck on something small, such as where to get keys cut or how to pay a bill, asking a local beats an hour of searching online.

FAQ

What should I sort out in my first month in Bangkok?

A Thai SIM, LINE and a ride-hailing app, a home near a rail station, and one weekly activity where you see the same people.

What do I need to buy a Thai SIM card as a foreigner?

Your passport, which is used to register the SIM. You can buy one at the airport or at a mobile operator's shop.

What is TM30?

It is the report of a foreigner's address to Thai immigration. The landlord normally files it; tenants should ask to confirm it has been done.

How do I start making friends as a newcomer in Bangkok?

Join something that repeats weekly, such as a run club, board-game café or language exchange, and add one friend app.
