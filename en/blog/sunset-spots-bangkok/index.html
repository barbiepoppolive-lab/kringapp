Best Sunset Spots in Bangkok, Mostly Free

Most of Bangkok's best sunset spots are along the Chao Phraya, above all Chao Phraya Sky Park on the Phra Pok Klao Bridge, free and open until 8pm. On the Sukhumvit side, the skywalks of Benjakitti Forest Park give an equally lovely evening view of the towers.

Why sunset makes the best plan

A sunset plan has a clear start time, does not take long, costs little and gives you plenty to talk about: the view, the sky, the photos. Afterwards you can carry on or head home easily, which makes it ideal with new friends.

Chao Phraya Sky Park

  • Built on the Phra Pok Klao Bridge over the abandoned structure of the unfinished Lavalin Skytrain, 280 metres long, linking a park on the old-town side with Chaloem Phrakiat Park on the Thonburi side.
  • Open daily 05:00–20:00, free.
  • Near MRT Sanam Chai and the Pak Khlong Talat and Saphan Phut piers.
  • Arrive half an hour before sunset for a good spot and long views both ways along the river.

The Thonburi riverside

The Thonburi bank has several riverside decks that are quieter than the old-town side. Sit looking back across the water as the light fades and watch the riverside towers light up one by one. Walk over from Chao Phraya Sky Park or take a cross-river ferry.

Talat Noi and Charoen Krung

Explore Talat Noi in the late afternoon, find a riverside corner in the warm late-afternoon light, then take a Chao Phraya Express Boat after dark to see the bridges and riverside buildings lit up. Route in Talat Noi and Charoen Krung.

The Benjakitti skywalks

If you are on the Sukhumvit side, Benjakitti Forest Park has elevated walkways over its wetlands, plus the 1.3 km Green Bridge to Lumphini Park. In the evening, orange light bounces off the towers around the park. Close to BTS Asok and MRT Queen Sirikit National Convention Centre.

The Asiatique Ferris wheel

For a high view without a skyscraper, the Asiatique Sky Ferris Wheel is 60 metres tall and a ride lasts about 15–20 minutes, with the river and city all around. Asiatique opens 16:00 to midnight, with a free shuttle boat from Sathorn Pier. The wheel itself is paid; check the price on site.

Rooftop viewpoints

Bangkok has several paid observation decks on tall towers. The views are spectacular, but prices are high and change often, so check prices and dress codes on the official websites before going. For a first hangout with new friends, the free riverside spots are usually more relaxed.

Sunset photo tips

  • Check sunset time in a weather app; in Bangkok it is around 6pm for most of the year.
  • Stay 15–20 minutes after the sun drops; the sky is often at its best then.
  • In the rainy season, the sky after a storm is extra clear and colourful.
  • Shoot a short video rather than a still; it captures the mood far better.

A sunset plan with new friends

Allow for travel time, because the sun will not wait. Gather somewhere easy to find, such as the entrance to Chao Phraya Sky Park, so latecomers still make it. Take turns taking photos of the group, then wander on to a night market together. That low-pressure rhythm keeps things comfortable for everyone.

After dark

  • Sky Park closes at 20:00. Leave before then and walk back along the main, well-lit roads.
  • Taking a boat on? Check the last departure first, or you may wait a long time.
  • With people you have just met, make your own way home and message each other when you are back.

FAQ

Where can I watch the sunset in Bangkok for free?

Chao Phraya Sky Park, the riverside decks on the Thonburi side and the skywalks in Benjakitti Forest Park are all free.

What time does Chao Phraya Sky Park close?

The latest listing says it is open daily from 05:00 to 20:00.

What time is sunset in Bangkok?

Around 6pm for most of the year. Check the exact time in a weather app.

Is there a high viewpoint without going up a tower?

Yes, the Asiatique Sky Ferris Wheel is 60 metres tall, and a ride lasts about 15–20 minutes.
