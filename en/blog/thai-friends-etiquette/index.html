Making Thai Friends: Etiquette, Culture and Phrases That Help

Making Thai friends is mostly about warmth, patience and a few cultural basics: be polite, never lose your temper in public and learn a handful of Thai phrases. Then go where Thais already want to know foreigners, such as language exchanges and run clubs.

Updated 17 Nov 2026 · 3 min read

Three ideas that explain a lot

  • Kreng jai (เกรงใจ): being considerate to the point of not wanting to impose. Thai friends may not tell you directly if something bothers them, or may hesitate to ask for help. Read between the lines, and make it easy for them to say no.
  • Mai pen rai (ไม่เป็นไร): "never mind, it's fine". Staying calm when things go wrong is valued; raising your voice in public costs you respect.
  • Sanuk (สนุก): fun. Shared laughter and doing things together, often in groups, is the heart of Thai friendship.

Etiquette that matters

  • The wai: the palms-together greeting. You do not need to start one with friends your age, but returning a wai, especially to someone older, is appreciated.
  • Heads and feet: the head is considered high and the feet low. Do not touch people's heads, and do not point your feet at people or at Buddha images.
  • The royal family: do not joke about it. It is a serious legal matter in Thailand, not just etiquette.
  • Seniority: age matters. Older friends may be called "phi" (พี่) and younger ones "nong" (น้อง). Using them shows warmth.
  • Nicknames: most Thais go by a short nickname, such as Mint, Ton or Ploy. Ask for it; it is friendlier than a full name.
  • Paying: in Thai groups the older or higher-earning person sometimes pays. Offer to split, but accept graciously if someone treats you, and return the favour next time.

Thai phrases to start with

Add "khrap" (ครับ) at the end if you are male. Women add "kha": a falling-tone ค่ะ after statements (sawasdee kha) and a high-tone คะ after questions (sabai dee mai kha?). It makes everything polite.

  • Sawasdee khrap / kha (สวัสดีครับ/ค่ะ, said roughly "sa-wat-dee"): hello.
  • Khop khun khrap / kha (ขอบคุณครับ/ค่ะ): thank you.
  • Sabai dee mai? (สบายดีไหม): how are you?
  • Chue arai khrap / kha? (ชื่ออะไรครับ/คะ): what's your name? Friendlier than the textbook "khun chue arai".
  • Chue len arai? (ชื่อเล่นอะไร): what is your nickname?
  • Mai pen rai (ไม่เป็นไร): no problem, never mind.
  • Gin khao rue yang? (กินข้าวหรือยัง): "have you eaten yet?", a friendly everyday greeting.

Thai is tonal, so expect to be misunderstood at first. Laugh about it; Thais usually love that you are trying.

Where to find Thai friends

  • Language exchanges: one Thai–English group has run free Sunday-afternoon sessions in Lumphini Park, with time split between the two languages. Check its latest listing before going.
  • Run clubs: popular with young Thais, many free. See running in Bangkok.
  • Classes: Muay Thai, cooking or ceramics bring Thais and foreigners together.
  • Neighbourhood spots: be a regular in Ari or Talat Noi, where many young Thais spend weekends.

Communication tips

  • Thais can be indirect. "Maybe" or "I'll see" often means no; do not push.
  • Group chats on LINE are where plans happen. If someone adds you to one, you are in.
  • Photos matter. Being happy to take and be in group photos is part of the fun.
  • Avoid heated debates about politics or religion with new friends.

Safety, both ways

Make friends in public places first. Be wary of anyone, of any nationality, who quickly brings up money, investments or loans.

FAQ

What is kreng jai?

A Thai value of being considerate and not wanting to impose on others. It is why Thai friends may not say directly when something bothers them.

Do I need to speak Thai to make Thai friends?

No. Many young Bangkok Thais speak some English, and a few Thai phrases plus patience go a long way. Translation in chat apps also helps.

What should I avoid doing with Thai friends?

Losing your temper in public, touching people's heads, pointing your feet at people and joking about the royal family.

Where can foreigners find Thai friends in Bangkok?

Language exchanges, run clubs, classes such as Muay Thai or cooking, and by becoming a regular at spots in areas like Ari and Talat Noi.
