Song Wat Road is the world's coolest street: go this week

Time Out has just ranked Bangkok's Song Wat Road the coolest street on Earth for 2026, and Song Wat Week lands right after it, 19–22 November. Here is why an old riverside trading street beat London and Buenos Aires, where to stop, and how to get there.

By the Kring team · Updated 18 Nov 2026 · 5 min read

✓ Hours, prices and transport on this page last checked 18 Nov 2026 · we re-check this page every month

How Song Wat beat the world

On 17 November 2026, Time Out published its list of the 28 coolest streets in the world for 2026, and Song Wat Road in Bangkok's Chinatown took first place. Second was Golborne Road in London, third Fernández de Enciso in Buenos Aires and fourth Vaasankatu in Helsinki. Time Out Bangkok put it simply: the street "now beats every street on the planet".

The list starts with nominations from Time Out's editors and writers in cities around the world. A panel of editors then scores each street on food and drink, shopping, nightlife, street life, community and local businesses.

What makes Song Wat special, to us, is not that it is new. It is that it still works for a living. Sacks of coffee beans, roasted peanuts and spices sit outside family wholesalers, and owners who have been here for decades still sit out front with a glass of iced tea. The coffee roasters, galleries and tiny bars that opened more recently moved in next door instead of pushing the old shops out. Old and new share the same wall here, and that is rare.

It is not Song Wat's first time on a Time Out list either. In 2023 it came 39th among the world's coolest neighbourhoods, and this year Talat Noi, five minutes' walk south, came 14th in the 2026 coolest neighbourhoods list.

A road the king sketched himself

Song Wat runs for about 1.2 kilometres along the Chao Phraya River on the Chinatown side. It was laid out under King Rama V after a big fire in Sampheng, and the name roughly means that the king sketched its line on the map with his own hand.

Through most of the 20th century the road was lined with warehouses, gold shops and the shophouses of trading families. Many of those shophouses still stand, which is why almost every corner photographs well. In 2022 local businesses formed a group called Made in Song Wat. It began with 10 shops and now has about 60 members, and it is a big reason the street has something on through the year.

Walking Song Wat: where to stop

Shops here keep their own hours, and many close one or two days a week, so check their pages before you go.

  • The old wholesalers. Walk slowly past the sacks of coffee beans, nuts and spices. Ask before you photograph people or the inside of a shop, and keep clear of the delivery trolleys.
  • Local Boys Coffee Co. A roaster in an old shophouse with a serious following. On weekends, arrive before 10am if you want a seat.
  • Gu Long Bao. A bun shop that is around a hundred years old. Take a couple to eat as you walk.
  • PLAY art house. A former shoe warehouse with a deep-blue front, now a space for exhibitions that are free to enter.
  • ROA's upside-down elephants. A mural from the 2016 Bukruk festival in the riverside car park, with the art and the Chao Phraya in one frame.
  • Cafés with a river view. A few places have seats at the back over the water, such as Arabica Song Wat. Good for a break when the sun is high.

Landmarks along the way

Chinese, Muslim and Thai families have shared this road for well over a century, and you can see it in how close together their places of worship stand.

  • Wat Mangkon Kamalawat. The MRT station is named after it, so it makes an easy starting point.
  • Wat Pathum Khongkha. At the southern end of Song Wat, where the road joins Talat Noi.
  • Luang Kocha Itsahak Mosque and old Chinese shrines tucked into the side lanes nearby.

Dress modestly and keep your voice down when you step inside any of them.

How to get to Song Wat Road

  • MRT Blue Line to Wat Mangkon, Exit 1. Come out on Plaeng Nam Road, cross Yaowarat Road and walk towards the river. It takes about 10 minutes.
  • Chao Phraya Express Boat to Ratchawong Pier, at the northern end of Song Wat. Check that your boat stops at Ratchawong before you board.
  • Driving. Parking around Chinatown is hard. The MRT or the boat is easier.

Song Wat Week 2026: Thursday 19 to Sunday 22 November

Song Wat Week is back for four days along the road, with activities, creative projects and collaborations between local shops and brands. Last year's highlight was a long shared table where 21 restaurants and cafés on the street told the area's story through their dishes. When this year's edition was announced in early October, the programme had not been released yet, so check the organisers' latest schedule before you go.

The street was named the world's best on Tuesday and the festival opens on Thursday. Timing like that does not come round often. If you were going to walk Song Wat once this year, make it this week. Expect more people than usual; Thursday or Friday afternoon will be easier than the weekend.

Tips before you go

  • Wear comfortable shoes. The pavements are narrow and delivery traffic runs all day.
  • Come in the afternoon to see the wholesalers still trading and the evening light on the river.
  • This is a working street. Walk gently and ask before photographing people or shop interiors.
  • Carry on south to Talat Noi, or head back up to Yaowarat when the neon comes on.

FAQ

What rank is Song Wat Road on Time Out's list?

No.1 of 28 streets worldwide in Time Out's coolest streets in the world for 2026, published on 17 November 2026.

Which MRT station is closest to Song Wat Road?

Wat Mangkon on the Blue Line. Take Exit 1 and walk about 10 minutes, or take the Chao Phraya Express Boat to Ratchawong Pier.

When is Song Wat Week 2026?

Thursday 19 to Sunday 22 November 2026, along Song Wat Road. Check the organisers' latest schedule before you go.

How long does a Song Wat walk take?

The road is about 1.2 km long. Allow two to three hours with stops, or half a day if you add Talat Noi.
